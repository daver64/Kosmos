# Kosmos 0.45

- File ▸ Set time to T-5:00 before burn keeps re-planning for more passes, so long waits (Mars, Jupiter) end at the lead time instead of well short of it.
- The lead time for Set time to before burn is never less than five minutes. A lead of 0 skipped right onto the ignition, and the plan could then slip past it to the next window (days later).
- OrbitComp PLN warns in red when the active stage does not have the dV the plan needs ("NOT ENOUGH dV"), and AUTO will not arm for it. An outer-planet transfer such as Uranus needs several km/s more than a Deltaglider carries, so the burn ran dry and the ship fell far short.
- OrbitComp ORB and MAN keep your ship's dot on the display: the plot scales to include the ship (an escape or heliocentric path used to push it off the screen), and any marker still out of range is pinned to the edge of the plot.
- OrbitComp PLN ejections have a FAST button: choose FLIGHT or DEPART and adjust the flight time (10 days a press) or the departure date (1 day a press) with + and -. A Lambert arc about the star replaces the Hohmann ellipse, so a quick transfer to Jupiter or beyond can be planned, and TO MAN and AUTO work from it.
- OrbitComp MAN draws the Sun-centred arrival when the node's escape orbit meets the target: both planets' orbits, your path, and you and the target at closest approach. Adjusting the node moves the track closer to or farther from the target.
- OrbitComp's Lambert intercept (PLN, for eccentric or inclined orbits in one frame) failed with a script error and showed nothing; it works now.
- OrbitComp's FAST has a BEST mode: FIND searches the next synodic period for the departure date and flight time with the lowest departure speed, for a flight no longer than the one set.
- The MFD manual has a complete beginner's tutorial for OrbitComp (glossary, Hohmann and fast plans, checking the arrival on MAN, flying the burn, trimming, troubleshooting).
- KosmosCAD opens ships whose parts are glTF/GLB models (for example the Voyager addon). Before, it reported a GLB error and showed nothing; now the model loads as editable meshes, and saving writes them back as OBJ.
- OrbitComp's target list is shorter: it offers the central star, the planets and the quantum portal, plus the moons, stations and ships that orbit the same body you do.
- The Systems MFD has a LOADS page: pick any instrument, heater or computer and switch it off or on, SHED everything non-essential in one press, and see how long the battery lasts. Both pages show the battery endurance.
- Unmanned craft have real loads you can switch in the Systems MFD (LOADS) or F1 window. Luna E3 is now a Luna 3 style probe: solar cells and batteries instead of an RTG, with the camera, film scanner and three-axis orientation system starting off. The ATV, Fregat, Transtage, Centaur G and Lunar Rover split their single 'Avionics' load into separate guidance, telemetry, thermal, sensor and thruster loads (same total demand), and the ATV's solar array is cut to match its real 4.8 kW.
- The Rayleigh haze over the sunlit surface now fades in from 12% of the planet's radius instead of only within 0.3%, so low orbit views of Earth, Venus and Titan get the hazed look instead of a bare surface.
- Earth no longer turns dull and muddy when you descend below the altitude where the terrain tiles take over (about 130 km). The tiles now get the same blue atmospheric haze as the distant globe, fading out towards the ground.
- Planet atmospheres are shaded the way Orbiter does it: light scattered towards the camera along each line of sight (blue over Earth, ochre over Mars), with the sunlight reddened by its slant path through the air. You get a bright hazed horizon, an orange terminator and a graded blue limb in place of the old flat blue veil. Earth's terrain tiles get the same haze, which fades out towards the ground.
- Airless bodies (the Moon, Mercury, asteroids) use a rough-surface shading with a bright full face and an opposition surge instead of the smooth lit-ball look. It needs the matching simlib.
- The haze now covers the gas giants too, with a bright limb and no wash over the day side.
- Earth's night lights were invisible from orbit (the texture's mip levels averaged the cities away) and missing below the globe-to-tile altitude. They now show from low orbit out to the whole globe, and flat damaged blocks in the source image are ignored.
- New --cam-planet R and --cam-phase DEG options point the camera at the planet only (R body radii out, DEG from the Sun, 0 = full face), for repeatable screenshots.
- Earth clouds now use the high-resolution Orbiter cloud tiles (tiled, sharp from orbit down to low altitude).
- Hi-res Mercury, Titan, Io, Europa, Enceladus, Phobos and Vesta textures; Io and Titan get Orbiter terrain.
- Venus and Titan now have a thick-atmosphere haze.
- The ocean sun glint on the distant Earth globe now fades in smoothly with altitude.
- New tool `tools/orbiter_tiles.py` unpacks Orbiter tile trees into a tile pyramid.
