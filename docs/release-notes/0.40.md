# Kosmos 0.40

- Performance: scenes with many ships, such as the orbital stations scenario, simulate about twice as fast. Unoccupied vessels no longer step their cabin gas grid once the air has settled, docking checks skip ships that are too far apart to couple, and the sphere-of-influence lookup is cheaper.
- Performance (simlib update): environment queries (gravity, air density, altitude) no longer copy every body's state on each call, propellant depletion and gear loads avoid the same copies, orbits of bodies on rails are solved once per substep, and the gas grid builds its face and vent lists once per step. Needs the matching simlib build.
- Apollo and Soyuz life support is now real-world specification. The Saturn V's command and service module is two stages: the service module carries the three fuel cells (4.2 kW), two cryogenic oxygen tanks (295 kg) and two hydrogen tanks (25 kg) that run the ship for the 14-day mission, and the command module has its own entry batteries, a small oxygen reserve, potable water and LiOH canisters. Both keep the crew alive until the service module is jettisoned, and the command module carries them to splashdown. The cabin is 6.2 m3 of pure oxygen at 34.5 kPa (5 psi). The Soyuz TMA has a 9 m3 cabin at sea-level air pressure with five days of oxygen, nitrogen and CO2 sorbent (3.2 days of free flight is the real figure); its solar arrays, batteries and gas bottles are in the service module and its descent batteries and reserve oxygen in the descent module.
- Low-pressure cabins are judged against their own design pressure, so a 34.5 kPa Apollo cabin no longer raises a LOW PRESSURE alarm.
- The command-line parser is now table driven (the long if/else chain that stressed MSVC is gone). The options and their behaviour are unchanged.
- Internal: the application state and the ship code are split into smaller, focused modules. No change in behaviour.
